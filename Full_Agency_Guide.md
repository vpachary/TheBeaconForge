## 0. Read This First — Honest Framing

Your real constraints: part-time (learning data analytics + job hunting), near-zero cash, delivering with AI, not fully fluent in English, need income in 1–3 months. These are workable if respected, fatal if you pretend you're a funded agency.

The core reframe:
- You are NOT starting a "web agency" on day one. You're starting a lead-gen + productized-service operation that BECOMES an agency once you have recurring revenue.
- The one-time website is the door-opener. The real business is the monthly retainer (hosting, maintenance, Google Business optimization, AI receptionist). Recurring revenue = the actual business.
- First ~10 clients: YOU stay close to delivery and at least review client comms. A commission salesperson before you have proof and case studies is wasted money.

The four numbers that define this:
- **₹20 lakh** — the GST registration threshold (the number that governs you early — NOT any "₹40 lakh Udyam limit," which doesn't exist).
- **$800–$2,500** — realistic one-time price for a local-business site to US/CA/AU.
- **$99–$300/mo** — the retainer per client. 30 retainers × $150 = $4,500/mo recurring. That's the goal.
- **2–5%** — realistic cold-email reply-to-close rate at the start. Plan outreach volume around it.

## 1. The Business Model

Find local businesses in wealthy English-speaking countries with no website or an outdated one → pitch a modern website + AI tools → deliver fast with AI → keep them on a monthly retainer.

Why local businesses, not tech startups:
- Startups judge you on portfolio depth — you lose there at the start.
- Local businesses (dentists, plumbers, law offices, gyms, med-spas, contractors) have money, clear pain, low tech-sophistication. You win on speed and being reachable.
- The catch: they're slow, price-sensitive, and ghost. Beat it with VOLUME of outreach + a tight niche so word-of-mouth compounds.

Blunt truth about "AI does everything": AI builds ~60–70% of a site fast. The last 30% (revisions, hosting, domain/DNS, bug fixes, "move the logo," timezone calls) eats the time you don't have part-time. Clients pay for reliability and accountability, which AI doesn't provide — you do.

## 8. Finding & Acquiring Clients

Lead source: Google Maps (businesses with no/weak website). Outreach: email + LinkedIn + contact form. **Never cold-call/SMS US numbers.**

Lead engine, step by step:
1. Pick niche + city (e.g., plumbers in Austin, TX).
2. Pull the list from Google Maps (name, category, phone, website field, rating, reviews). Tools: Apify / Google Places API / a Maps scraper. Note: scraping violates Google's TOS — private lead source only, expect to rotate tools.
3. Filter to no-website / broken-mobile sites — your hottest leads.
4. Find the owner's email (contact page, Facebook, email-finder).
5. Record a personal hook (their rating, a specific flaw).
6. Send a personalized cold email + free Loom audit (Section 9).
7. Follow up 3–4× over 2 weeks (most replies come from follow-ups).
8. Book a call / async video → close on GROWTH → 50% upfront.

Channel ranking for a beginner: cold email (Maps leads) → LinkedIn → Loom audits (send with the email) → Upwork (backup for fast cash + first case studies) → referrals (slow then compounds) → paid ads (skip until cash + proof).


Sequence: Day 0 email+Loom → Day 3 "did you watch?" → Day 7 share an example → Day 12 break-up email ("should I close your file?" — often gets the reply).

The close: ask about their problem before pitching ("How do you get customers now? What happens when you miss a call?") → mirror the pain → present three packages, recommend GROWTH → handle price by anchoring to lost revenue ("one missed $2,000 job pays for a year of this") → "I can start once the 50% deposit's in — want the invoice?"

On the salesperson (your plan) — order matters:
- WRONG: hire a commission closer day one with no portfolio/proof. Good closers won't take it; bad ones burn your leads.
- RIGHT: you (or a cheap VA) do async outreach (email + Loom = fluency-safe) for the first ~5–10 clients. Build 3 case studies.
- THEN: hire a native-English closer on commission (10–20% of first payment + a slice of retainer). Now they multiply you instead of costing you.

## 10. Outreach Compliance (Two Different Laws)

**CAN-SPAM (EMAIL only).** Cold-emailing a US business is legal — no prior consent needed, even for a scraped email — IF every email has:
1. Real sender (real name + real you@youragency.com).
2. Honest subject line.
3. Real physical postal address in the footer.
4. Working unsubscribe, honored within 10 days.

Footer:
```
[Your Agency Name]
[Real street address], [City], India
Don't want these emails? Reply "unsubscribe" and I'll remove you.
```
Do NOT put scraped emails into Brevo/Mailchimp (their terms ban cold lists — you'll get banned). A tool doesn't make you compliant — the 4 items do.

**TCPA (CALLS & TEXTS).** CAN-SPAM doesn't cover phone. Cold-calling a DNC/cell number or cold-texting a scraped number = $500–$1,500 per call/text. From India you can't tell which numbers are cells or on the DNC list. **Never cold-call or cold-text scraped Maps numbers.** Only call after they reply and agree.

What to do with scraped Maps data:

| Scraped | Action | Legal? |
|---|---|---|
| Email | Cold email (4 rules) | Yes |
| Email | Bulk tool (Brevo/Mailchimp) | No — terms ban it |
| Phone | Cold call | Risky — TCPA, avoid |
| Phone | Cold text | No — TCPA |
| Phone | Call after they agreed | Yes — consent |

Safe playbook: scrape → email + LinkedIn + contact form → they reply → then call if needed.

Privacy without exposing your home: LinkedIn/contact-forms/DMs need NO postal address — start there for ₹0. For cold email, use a trusted contact's address (free) or a virtual mailbox (US ~$10–30/mo, India ~₹400–800/mo) once you have your first client. No legit *free* US virtual address exists — don't use one you don't control. Never fake a US identity.

## 11. India-Side: Payments, Tax, Contracts

**Myth-buster:** there is NO "₹40 lakh limit via Udyam." Udyam (MSME) is a classification/benefits scheme — micro = turnover up to ₹10 crore (2025 rules), export turnover even excluded. Not a bank cap. The number that governs you early is the **₹20 lakh GST threshold**.

Receiving money from abroad (legally):
- Open a current account in your business name (Udyam + PAN + Aadhaar to start).
- Receive USD via Wise Business / Payoneer / SWIFT (converts to INR into your account).
- Get an **FIRC / FIRA / e-BRC** for each payment — legal FEMA proof it's an export earning. Wise/Payoneer + your bank provide it.

GST (the part that matters):
- Export of services is **zero-rated** — effectively no GST on foreign invoices, if conditions met (you in India, client abroad, paid in foreign currency).
- Below ₹20 lakh turnover you may not need to register. But to formally claim zero-rated export benefit, most export freelancers register anyway.
- If registered, file an **LUT (Form RFD-11)** each financial year — export without paying IGST upfront (no blocked capital). Renew every April.
- Income tax is separate and always applies on profit. Keep every invoice + FIRC.

Contracts: written agreement per client — scope, price, 50% deposit, revision cap, ownership-on-final-payment, retainer terms, cancellation notice. A clear scope + revision cap is your #1 protection against margin-killing "just one more change."

**See a CA at your FIRST foreign payment (or before) — NOT at "₹40 lakh" (too late, and the limit's a myth).** One consult (~₹2,000–5,000) sets up GST/LUT/bookkeeping right and prevents an expensive mess. This is general info, not tax/legal advice.

US LLC: skip it for the first 6–12 months. You can invoice + get paid via Wise/Payoneer without one. An LLC adds cost + mandatory annual US tax filings (Form 5472/1120 — $25,000 penalty per missed form for non-residents). Revisit only when steady US revenue justifies it, and do it with a CA/US accountant — never a "free LLC" click-through.

## 13. Delivery Process & Hosting

7-day delivery flow:
1. Deposit (50%) → send intake form.
2. Generate first draft with AI + template (Day 1–2).
3. Send preview + Loom walkthrough (Day 3).
4. One round of revisions (Day 4–5).
5. Connect domain, hosting, install AI chat (Day 6).
6. Go live, collect final 50%, start retainer clock (Day 7).

Hosting decision tree:
- **Static local-biz site (the common case)** → Cloudflare Pages / Netlify (free tier, fast US/AU CDN). Forms via Netlify Forms/Formspree; booking via Cal.com/Calendly embed; AI chat via Chatbase/Voiceflow embed; payments via Stripe hosted checkout. No real backend needed.
- **WordPress / real backend needed** → Cloudways or Hostinger (PHP host), domain routed through Cloudflare for speed/security.
- Two rules: (1) host under YOUR account, client pays you monthly — if they own hosting they can walk and keep the site, retainer dies. (2) Pick a US/AU data center, not India, so the client's visitors get fast load.

### Handover & Ownership (how to avoid trouble after the site is done)

Run **Model A**: you build + maintain (retainer), client owns their core accounts, you keep the leverage. Handover means giving them *use*, not surrendering your leverage.

The client-owned project Gmail setup (recommended):
- Client creates a **new dedicated Gmail for the project** and owns it (their recovery phone/email on it).
- Domain, Google Business Profile, and Analytics are set up under that client-owned Gmail → the client clearly owns their business identity, so you can never be accused of holding accounts hostage.
- You get the password to operate it day-to-day.
- **Your leverage is NOT that Gmail login** (the client can reset it and lock you out). Your leverage is: (1) the **website hosting under YOUR own hosting account**, and (2) the **contract**. Stop-paying → you can suspend the hosted site per contract.
- Turn on 2FA with the *client's* phone as recovery (it's their account); keep the password in your own password manager so you're not casually locked out. Never store your personal data in that Gmail.

Safe handover process:
1. **Final 50% clears BEFORE full handover.** Show the live site on your hosting first ("here it is, working") → payment in → then give access. Biggest single protection.
2. **Ownership transfers only on full payment** (contract clause below).
3. Give a limited/editor login for content, keep master control while they pay.
4. **Documented handover**: a short email/doc listing what's live, the URL, what login they have, what the retainer includes and what it does NOT (kills "I thought that was included" fights) + a quick Loom walkthrough.
5. **Get written approval back**: "Received and approved, site is live and working." Kills future "it never worked" chargeback disputes.

Two specific traps:
- **Domain ownership** — keep the domain under the client-owned Gmail (their account), you just manage DNS. Owning their domain yourself invites "hostage" accusations. Clean split: they own domain, you own hosting/build.
- **Chargebacks** — international clients can dispute a payment months later. Protect with: signed contract + their written final approval + invoices + payment records = proof of delivery.

Reusable contract clauses (plain English — have a CA/lawyer confirm for your jurisdiction):
- *Ownership:* "All deliverables and intellectual property remain the property of [Agency] until payment is received in full. Upon full payment, ownership of the final website transfers to the Client."
- *Project account:* "Client provides and owns a dedicated Google account for the project. [Agency] is granted operational access for build and maintenance. Access may be revoked by either party on [X] days' notice per the cancellation terms."
- *Hosting/retainer:* "The website is hosted and maintained by [Agency] under the monthly plan. If the monthly plan lapses beyond [X] days, [Agency] may suspend hosting until payment resumes."
- *Revisions/scope:* "[N] rounds of revisions are included. Additional changes are billed at $[Y]/hour or covered under the active retainer."
- *Payment:* "50% deposit before work begins; remaining 50% due before final handover. Ongoing plan billed monthly in advance."

Handover checklist (per client):
- [ ] Final 50% received
- [ ] Site live on your hosting, tested on mobile
- [ ] Accounts set up under client-owned project Gmail (domain, Google Business, Analytics)
- [ ] Client given limited editor login (you keep master + hosting)
- [ ] Handover email sent (URL, logins, what's in/out of retainer)
- [ ] Loom walkthrough sent
- [ ] Written "approved & live" reply received and saved
- [ ] Contract + invoices + approval filed (chargeback protection)
- Retainer value when hosting is nearly free = what you *manage*: edits, backups, security, AI chat, SEO, uptime. Bundle those so $149/mo is clearly justified.

Tool stack (start free/cheap): builder (Framer / Webflow / WordPress+Elementor / Carrd) · AI (Claude, ChatGPT) · AI chat (Chatbase, Voiceflow, Tidio) · AI voice (Vapi, Retell, Bland) · cold email (Instantly, Smartlead, or manual Gmail) · scraping (Apify, Places API) · Loom · invoicing (Wise/Payoneer, Zoho) · pipeline (Notion or Trello).

Recommended builder for you: **Framer** — hosting built-in, fast, modern, nothing separate to babysit (matters when part-time). Move to WordPress only when a client needs it.

CRM note: don't self-host SuiteCRM yet — it's a system to maintain before you have leads, and its email module doesn't fix cold-email deliverability. Start with Notion/Trello for pipeline + Instantly/Smartlead for sending. Add SuiteCRM later at real volume. Never send cold email from a self-hosted SMTP (spam problems).

## 14. Freelancers vs Contractors vs Employees

Keep it variable-cost. No fixed salaries you must pay whether or not clients come in.

Hiring order for you:
1. You + AI deliver clients 1–5, do written+Loom outreach yourself.
2. Add ONE freelance web builder (per-project) when delivery becomes the bottleneck.
3. THEN a commission salesperson to scale outreach once you have proof.
4. Employees only when recurring revenue covers salaries for 6+ months.

Margins: solo+AI 80–90%; with freelancers 40–60% per project (you keep client + retainer); retainers near pure margin once set up.

## 16. Scaling: First Client → Stable Business

1. Clients 1–3: do everything yourself. Goal = 3 case studies + testimonials. Proof is the asset, money is secondary.
2. Clients 4–10: systematize — templatize the build, intake form, delivery Loom, contract. Same niche only.
3. Clients 10–20: add a freelance builder (stop being the bottleneck). Push retainers hard.
4. Now hire the commission salesperson. Feed them proof + scripts.
5. Clients 20–50: productize fully, raise prices, add a second niche/city, VA for lead-gen ops.
6. Year 2: recurring revenue covers costs; reinvest in ads + a small team. Real, stable agency.

The one metric: **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR).** One-time builds pay this month's bills; MRR makes the business worth something. Every decision asks "does this grow MRR?" First real milestone: **$5,000 MRR** (~30 retainers).
